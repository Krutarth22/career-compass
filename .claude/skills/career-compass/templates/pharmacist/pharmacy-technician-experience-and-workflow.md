# Pharmacy Technician Experience and Dispensing Workflow

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Obtaining technician registration or certification
2. Working in a dispensing workflow under pharmacist supervision
3. Applying error-prevention practices at every step
4. Handling inventory, controlled substances, and insurance processes
5. Reflecting on the pharmacist's clinical role observed

## What You'll Build
A pharmacy experience portfolio: technician registration or
certification per your jurisdiction, at least one hundred fifty hours
in a community or hospital pharmacy with a documented log, a
dispensing workflow map with the error-prevention checks at each
step, a record of the pharmacist interventions you observed
(interaction catches, dose corrections, counseling), an analysis of
three published dispensing error cases, shadowing reflections on
pharmacist roles in two settings, and a supervisor evaluation.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Technician requirements vary; complete what your jurisdiction
  requires before handling medications.
- Observe pharmacist clinical decisions deliberately; they are the
  point of the experience for a future pharmacist.
- Privacy rules apply to every record and reflection.

## Steps
1. Complete technician registration or certification and any required
   training, and log it.
2. Secure a technician position or a supervised volunteer role and
   complete onboarding.
3. Map the dispensing workflow from prescription receipt to pickup,
   marking the verification and error-prevention points.
4. Log hours and duties weekly with the setting and supervisor.
5. Record pharmacist interventions you observe with the reasoning,
   de-identified.
6. Analyze three published dispensing error cases for contributing
   factors and the safeguards that failed or would have helped.
7. Shadow a pharmacist in a second setting (hospital, clinic,
   ambulatory care) and reflect on the differences in role.
8. Obtain a supervisor evaluation and summarize what the experience
   taught you about pharmacy as a career.

## Extension Ideas
- Obtain a sterile compounding technician credential.
- Complete an immunization technician training if available.
- Interview pharmacists in three practice areas.
- Learn the pharmacy's inventory and controlled-substance
  reconciliation process in detail.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Technician-level pharmacy operations
- Dispensing workflow and error-prevention understanding
- Observation of pharmacist clinical practice
- Reflective professional development

## Credential Milestones
- Pre-pharmacy prerequisite coursework in progress (biology,
  chemistry, organic chemistry, biochemistry, anatomy and physiology,
  microbiology, calculus, statistics, economics); verify each
  program's list.
- Pharmacy technician registration or certification per state
  requirements; many programs value technician experience.
- Application through the central application service; some programs
  require an entrance examination or interview.
- Requirements differ by country and state; confirm for your
  jurisdiction.

## Industry Relevance

Community Pharmacies, Hospital Pharmacies, Pharmacy Schools, Pharmacy Benefit Organizations. Pharmacy programs and employers strongly prefer applicants with technician experience because it confirms understanding of the profession and provides operational competence. A portfolio with certification, logged hours, workflow analysis, and observed interventions is the foundation of a competitive pharmacy application.
