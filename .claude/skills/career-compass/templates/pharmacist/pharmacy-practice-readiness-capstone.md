# Pharmacy Practice Readiness Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Preparing for licensing examinations with measured practice
2. Assembling a portfolio for residency or first position applications
3. Demonstrating the pharmacist patient care process end to end
4. Preparing application materials and interviewing
5. Planning licensure, first year, and specialty certification

## What You'll Build
A practice readiness portfolio for your next stage (program
application, licensure and first position, or residency): application
materials (personal statement or letter of intent, curriculum vitae,
references tracked), a licensing examination preparation plan with
baseline and progress data over at least eight weeks (clinical and
law components), a competency evidence map linking portfolio items
(technician experience, drug information, medication reviews,
compounding, formulary work) to the competencies assessed, a
simulated full patient care encounter demonstrating the pharmacist
patient care process with documentation, two mock interviews with
feedback, and a two-year milestone plan with contingencies.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Tailor to your stage; the structure holds for applicants,
  graduating students, and residency candidates.
- Practice examination data must be real and tracked; include the
  jurisprudence component for your jurisdiction.
- Residency candidates should follow the application service's
  requirements and timelines.

## Steps
1. Identify the next stage and its competency framework and build the
   evidence map from your portfolio.
2. Draft the personal statement or letter of intent and the
   curriculum vitae and obtain feedback from a pharmacist.
3. Request references with a packet for each and track them.
4. Take baseline practice examinations for the clinical and law
   components, build the study plan, and track progress weekly.
5. Run the simulated patient care encounter (collect, assess, plan,
   implement, follow up) with documentation, and have it reviewed.
6. Complete two mock interviews in the target format with feedback.
7. Assemble the portfolio with materials, exam data, the encounter
   documentation, and interview feedback.
8. Write the two-year milestone plan with decision points and
   contingencies, and review it with a mentor.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a residency showcase preparation with a clinical case
  presentation.
- Obtain immunization and point-of-care testing certificates.
- Build a longitudinal portfolio for board certification.
- Present the portfolio to an advisor for a formal readiness review.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Measured licensing examination preparation
- Competency portfolio assembly
- Pharmacist patient care process demonstration
- Application, interviewing, and development planning

## Credential Milestones
- Pre-pharmacy: prerequisites, technician experience, central
  application, and admission to an accredited Doctor of Pharmacy
  program.
- Program: didactic coursework, introductory and advanced pharmacy
  practice experiences with required hours, and graduation.
- Licensure: the national pharmacist licensing examination (NAPLEX)
  and the jurisprudence examination for the state (MPJE or a
  state-specific examination), intern hours as required, and state
  licensure.
- Practice: employer credentialing, immunization certification, and
  compounding competencies as applicable.
- Postgraduate: residency through the central application service and
  the match, and board certification in a specialty after eligibility.
- Requirements differ by country and state; confirm for your
  jurisdiction.

## Industry Relevance

Pharmacy Schools, Hospital and Community Pharmacies, Residency Programs, Licensing Boards. Licensure and postgraduate placement are the decisive gates of the pharmacy pathway, and both reward demonstrated readiness with evidence. A complete readiness portfolio with a simulated care encounter and a milestone plan is the definitive artifact for the pharmacist pathway.
