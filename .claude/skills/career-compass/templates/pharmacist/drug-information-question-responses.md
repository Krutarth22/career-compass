# Drug Information Question Responses

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Clarifying a drug information request and its context
2. Searching tertiary, secondary, and primary sources systematically
3. Evaluating evidence quality and applicability
4. Writing a formal response with a clear recommendation
5. Documenting for the drug information record

## What You'll Build
A drug information portfolio of ten formal responses to realistic
questions from clinicians and patients (dosing in renal impairment,
interaction management, use in pregnancy, off-label evidence,
compatibility, adverse effect causality): for each, the clarified
question with background, the systematic search across tertiary,
secondary, and primary sources with documentation, an evidence
summary with appraisal, a written response in the standard format
with a recommendation and references, and a review by a pharmacist.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use references available through a library or free resources;
  document access limits.
- Follow a published systematic search approach and the standard
  response format.
- Ten questions across categories is the floor.

## Steps
1. Write ten realistic requests with requester type and clinical
   context, and clarify each into a searchable question.
2. Search tertiary references first and document findings and gaps.
3. Search secondary databases and retrieve primary literature for
   each question.
4. Appraise the primary literature for quality and applicability.
5. Synthesize the evidence and write the response: background,
   evidence summary, recommendation, and references.
6. Include monitoring, alternatives, and caveats where appropriate.
7. Obtain review from a pharmacist and revise.
8. Compile the portfolio with a log of questions, time spent, and
   sources, and a reflection on the search process.

## Extension Ideas
- Answer a question under a simulated urgent time limit.
- Write a patient-facing version of two responses in plain language.
- Build a drug information request tracking system.
- Complete a drug information rotation or elective.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Drug information request clarification
- Systematic literature searching across source tiers
- Evidence appraisal and synthesis
- Formal written responses with recommendations

## Credential Milestones
- Pharmacy students: drug information skills are assessed in didactic
  coursework and introductory practice experiences, and the
  literature evaluation content appears on the licensing examination.
- Pre-pharmacy applicants: demonstrates scientific literacy for
  interviews.
- Drug information residencies and specialty practice build on this
  foundation.

## Industry Relevance

Hospital Pharmacies, Drug Information Centers, Pharmaceutical Medical Affairs, Managed Care Organizations. Answering drug information questions with evidence is a defining pharmacist competency across these settings, and medical information roles in industry hire directly for it. A reviewed portfolio of formal responses demonstrates that competency with evidence.
