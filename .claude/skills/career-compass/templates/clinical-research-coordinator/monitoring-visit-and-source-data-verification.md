# Monitoring Visit and Source Data Verification

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Preparing a site for a monitoring visit
2. Performing source data verification against case report forms
3. Resolving data queries and documenting corrections properly
4. Writing monitoring findings and follow-up letters
5. Closing out findings and preventing recurrence

## What You'll Build
A two-sided monitoring exercise on your simulated study: as the site
coordinator, prepare for a visit and respond to findings; as the
monitor (with a peer alternating roles), conduct source data
verification of your simulated participants against entered data,
issue queries, write a monitoring visit report and follow-up letter,
and track findings to closure with corrective actions, plus a
comparison of what each role found.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Enter your simulated participants' data into your EDC (or a form
  set) so verification is real.
- Seed some discrepancies in the source or entered data.
- Both roles must be performed and documented.

## Steps
1. Prepare the site: an updated regulatory binder, source documents
   filed, the visit agenda, and staff availability.
2. As monitor, build the verification plan: which data points, which
   participants, and the tolerance for discrepancies.
3. Perform source data verification, recording every discrepancy with
   its category (transcription, missing source, protocol deviation,
   consent issue).
4. Issue queries in the EDC or on a query form with clear text.
5. As coordinator, resolve the queries with proper correction practice
   (single line, initials, date, reason) and respond.
6. As monitor, write the monitoring visit report and the follow-up
   letter with findings, required actions, and due dates.
7. As coordinator, implement corrective and preventive actions and
   document closure evidence.
8. Compare the two perspectives in a short note: what the site missed,
   what the monitor found, and how the process could improve.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a remote monitoring session with a shared electronic source.
- Perform a risk-based monitoring plan with reduced verification.
- Simulate an inspection using a regulatory inspection checklist.
- Build a site quality metrics dashboard.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Site preparation for monitoring
- Source data verification and query resolution
- Monitoring report and follow-up letter writing
- Corrective and preventive action management

## Industry Relevance

Contract Research Organizations, Sponsors, Clinical Research Sites, Academic Medical Centers. Coordinators who understand monitoring from both sides progress fastest into clinical research associate roles in these sectors, and the ability to write a clear monitoring report is directly assessed in those interviews. A two-sided exercise with reports and closure evidence demonstrates both perspectives.
