# Time Study and Assembly Line Balancing

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Breaking work into elements and timing them with proper method
2. Rating performance and applying allowances to get standard times
3. Building precedence diagrams and balancing to a takt time
4. Measuring line efficiency and identifying bottlenecks
5. Proposing and testing a rebalanced line

## What You'll Build
A work measurement and line balancing study on a real multi-step
assembly process with at least three workstations (a club's product
assembly, a small business, a volunteer kit-packing line, or a
process you set up with friends): element-level time studies with
enough cycles for statistical confidence, standard times with ratings
and allowances, a precedence diagram, a balanced line to a stated takt
with efficiency and smoothness index, a rebalanced proposal, and a
trial of the new balance with measured results.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Three stations with at least ten cycles timed per element is the
  floor.
- Video with consent makes element timing far more accurate.
- The rebalance must be tried, even if briefly, and measured.

## Steps
1. Define the process and its stations, get participant agreement, and
   write the study plan with the takt time from a stated demand.
2. Break the work into elements with clear start and stop points and
   build the observation sheet.
3. Time at least ten cycles per element, rate performance, and compute
   the number of cycles needed for the confidence you want.
4. Apply allowances and compute standard times per element; document
   the allowance basis.
5. Build the precedence diagram and compute the current line's
   efficiency, balance delay, and bottleneck.
6. Rebalance using a heuristic (largest candidate, ranked positional
   weight) to the takt time and compute the new efficiency and
   smoothness index.
7. Trial the rebalanced line, measure cycle times and output, and
   compare to prediction.
8. Write the report: method, data, standard times, balance analysis,
   the proposal, trial results, and recommendations.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a predetermined motion time system estimate and compare.
- Model variability with a simulation of the balanced line.
- Add ergonomic assessment of the most loaded station.
- Balance for a mixed-model sequence.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Element-level time study method
- Standard time development with ratings and allowances
- Precedence analysis and line balancing
- Trial-based validation of a proposal

## Industry Relevance

Electronics Assembly, Automotive, Consumer Goods, Fulfilment Centers. Work measurement and line balancing are foundational industrial engineering skills in these sectors and the basis of capacity and labor planning. A study with real timings and a trialed rebalance is the classic entry portfolio piece.
