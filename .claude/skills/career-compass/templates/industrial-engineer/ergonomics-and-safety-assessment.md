# Ergonomics and Safety Assessment of a Workstation

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Assessing physical risk factors with standard tools
2. Identifying hazards and evaluating risk
3. Designing controls in the hierarchy from elimination to PPE
4. Redesigning the workstation and validating the improvement
5. Documenting for safety compliance and training

## What You'll Build
An ergonomic and safety assessment of a real workstation (from your
time study or another process): a task analysis, an ergonomic risk
assessment with recognized tools (RULA or REBA for posture, the NIOSH
lifting equation where lifting exists), a hazard identification and
risk assessment with a matrix, controls proposed through the hierarchy
of controls, a redesigned workstation implemented or mocked up, a
reassessment showing reduced risk scores, and a safety documentation
package with a training outline.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Consent and care for the worker being observed are mandatory; video
  only with permission.
- Use the published tool worksheets and cite them.
- The redesign can be low cost (height adjustment, tool placement, a
  lift aid) but must be tried and reassessed.

## Steps
1. Observe and document the task: steps, durations, postures, forces,
   repetitions, and the environment.
2. Apply the posture assessment tool to the worst postures and, if
   lifting exists, the lifting equation; record the scores.
3. Identify hazards (mechanical, electrical, chemical, slips, noise)
   and assess risk with a matrix; cite the applicable regulations.
4. Propose controls for each risk factor and hazard using the
   hierarchy of controls, with costs and effects.
5. Redesign the workstation on paper and in a mock-up or implementation
   with the worker's input.
6. Reassess the redesigned task with the same tools and compare scores;
   measure any effect on cycle time.
7. Write the safety documentation: the assessment, the controls, a
   safe work procedure, and a training outline.
8. Present the findings to the process owner and the worker and record
   their feedback and commitments.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a job rotation schedule that balances exposure across workers.
- Measure noise and lighting and compare to standards.
- Perform a lockout-tagout procedure for a piece of equipment.
- Build an ergonomic assessment template for the whole facility.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Ergonomic risk assessment with standard tools
- Hazard identification and risk evaluation
- Control design through the hierarchy
- Workstation redesign validated by reassessment

## Industry Relevance

Manufacturing, Warehousing, Healthcare, Construction Support. Injury prevention is a regulatory and financial priority in these sectors, and industrial engineers are often the ergonomic and safety resource on site. A before-and-after assessment with a redesigned workstation is a directly relevant portfolio piece.
