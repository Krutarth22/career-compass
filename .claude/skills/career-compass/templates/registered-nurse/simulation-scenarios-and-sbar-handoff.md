# Clinical Simulation Scenarios and SBAR Handoff

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Recognizing a deteriorating patient from assessment cues
2. Prioritizing and acting within scope, including escalation
3. Communicating with providers using a structured format
4. Handing off safely at shift change
5. Debriefing to improve judgment and teamwork

## What You'll Build
A set of four simulation scenarios you run with peers (in a skills lab
if available, or a low-fidelity setup with a peer as patient and
scripted cues): a deteriorating post-operative patient, a hypoglycemic
episode, a chest pain presentation, and a fall with injury, each with
a scenario script, expected actions, an SBAR call to a provider
recorded and scored, a shift handoff using a structured tool, a
structured debrief with reflection, and a summary of the clinical
judgment patterns you improved across the four.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Low-fidelity simulation with scripted vital signs and cues is
  acceptable; the reasoning and communication are the point.
- Record the SBAR calls and score them against a rubric.
- Debriefing should follow a published structured method.

## Steps
1. Write the four scenario scripts with initial state, cue
   progression, expected nursing actions, and escalation points.
2. Prepare the simulation materials and brief the peer playing the
   patient and the observer.
3. Run each scenario, performing assessment, prioritization,
   interventions within scope, and escalation.
4. Make the SBAR call to a peer playing the provider, record it, and
   score it against the rubric.
5. Perform a structured shift handoff for the patient and have the
   receiving peer score completeness.
6. Debrief each scenario with the structured method and write a
   reflection on judgment and communication.
7. Repeat one scenario after remediation and compare scores.
8. Summarize the patterns across scenarios and the improvement
   evidence.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a code scenario using basic life support skills.
- Run an interprofessional simulation with a medical or pharmacy
  student.
- Use a simulation center's high-fidelity manikin if accessible.
- Build a scenario on a mental health crisis with de-escalation.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Recognition and response to patient deterioration
- Prioritization and escalation within scope
- Structured communication and handoff
- Reflective debriefing and improvement

## Credential Milestones
- Nursing students: simulation performance is assessed in clinical
  courses; the scenarios map to the NCLEX clinical judgment model
  (recognize, analyze, prioritize, generate, act, evaluate).
- Basic life support certification is required for clinical
  placements; advanced cardiac life support is expected in many
  acute care roles after licensure.
- New graduate nurse residency programs use similar simulations for
  competency validation.

## Industry Relevance

Hospitals, Nursing Schools, Simulation Centers, Nurse Residency Programs. Recognizing deterioration and communicating clearly are the competencies most associated with patient outcomes, and they are what nurse residency programs and preceptors evaluate first. A set of run scenarios with scored communication and debrief evidence demonstrates readiness for practice.
