# Unit-Based Nursing Quality Improvement Project

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Identifying a nursing-sensitive quality problem on a unit
2. Reviewing the evidence for effective practices
3. Implementing a change with the nursing team through PDSA cycles
4. Measuring outcomes with nursing-sensitive indicators
5. Reporting to unit leadership and sustaining the change

## What You'll Build
A quality improvement project on a unit or facility where you work or
train (falls, pressure injury prevention, catheter-associated
infection bundle compliance, hand hygiene, patient education): a
charter with an aim tied to a nursing-sensitive indicator, an
evidence review of effective practices, a process map and cause
analysis with staff input, at least two PDSA cycles with run charts,
staff engagement documented, a report and poster, and a presentation
to unit leadership with a sustainment plan.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Requires a host unit willing to support a small project; discuss
  with the nurse manager or a clinical instructor.
- Determine whether the facility requires a quality project review or
  ethics exemption and document it.
- Two cycles with real audit data is the floor.

## Steps
1. Meet the nurse manager or educator and identify a problem with an
   available indicator; write the charter with a SMART aim.
2. Review the evidence and guidelines for effective practices and
   summarize them for the team.
3. Map the current process and analyze causes with staff at a huddle.
4. Establish a baseline with audits over at least two weeks.
5. Run the first PDSA cycle with a change idea and a prediction,
   collect data, and study it on a run chart.
6. Adapt and run the second cycle, documenting staff engagement and
   feedback.
7. Analyze results, including any balancing measures, and write the
   SQUIRE-format report and poster.
8. Present to unit leadership and hand over the sustainment plan with
   an audit schedule.

## Extension Ideas
- Submit the poster to a nursing quality conference.
- Extend the project to a second unit with a spread plan.
- Add a patient-reported experience measure.
- Complete a recognized quality improvement certificate.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Quality improvement chartering on nursing-sensitive indicators
- Evidence review and translation to practice
- PDSA cycles with audit data and run charts
- Leadership reporting and sustainment

## Credential Milestones
- Nursing students: quality improvement and evidence-based practice
  are required program competencies and often a capstone requirement
  in baccalaureate programs.
- Practicing nurses: quality project leadership supports clinical
  ladder advancement and specialty certification portfolios.
- Graduate nursing programs expect evidence-based practice projects.

## Industry Relevance

Hospitals, Long-Term Care Facilities, Magnet-Designated Health Systems, Nursing Schools. Nursing-sensitive quality indicators are tracked and reported by health systems, and nurses who lead improvement are advanced into leadership. A completed unit project with data and a leadership presentation is the recognized evidence of that capability.
