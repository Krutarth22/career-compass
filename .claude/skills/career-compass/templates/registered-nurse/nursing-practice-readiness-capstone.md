# Nursing Practice Readiness Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Preparing for the licensing examination with measured practice
2. Assembling a competency portfolio for a preceptorship or residency
3. Managing a full patient assignment in simulation
4. Preparing application materials and interviewing
5. Planning licensure, first-year development, and specialty
   certification

## What You'll Build
A practice readiness portfolio: a licensing examination preparation
plan with baseline and progress data from practice assessments over
at least eight weeks, a competency evidence map linking portfolio
items (care plans, medication safety, simulation, quality
improvement) to the program's and employers' competency frameworks, a
simulated full-shift assignment of three patients with prioritization,
documentation, and handoffs, application materials (resume, cover
letter, references) for a nurse residency or first position, two mock
interviews with feedback, and a first-year development and
certification plan.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Tailor to your stage: pre-nursing applicants adapt the plan to
  program admission; students and new graduates to licensure and
  first employment.
- Practice assessment data must be real and tracked.
- The simulated shift can be run with peers using your earlier
  scenarios.

## Steps
1. Identify the next stage and its competency framework and build the
   evidence map from your portfolio.
2. Take a baseline practice assessment, build the study plan, and
   track weekly progress with remediation of weak areas.
3. Design and run the simulated three-patient shift with
   prioritization, time management, medication administration
   simulation, documentation, and end-of-shift handoffs; debrief.
4. Prepare the resume, cover letter, and reference list, and obtain
   feedback from a nurse.
5. Complete two mock interviews in the format employers use
   (behavioral, situational) with feedback.
6. Research nurse residency programs or first positions and build an
   application tracker.
7. Write the first-year development plan: orientation goals,
   preceptorship objectives, and a target specialty certification
   with its eligibility requirements.
8. Assemble the portfolio and review it with an educator or mentor.

## Extension Ideas
- Complete an advanced life support certification.
- Shadow in two specialties to inform the first-position choice.
- Prepare a teaching plan for a patient population you will serve.
- Build a longitudinal portfolio structure for career-long use.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Measured licensing examination preparation
- Competency portfolio assembly
- Full-assignment management in simulation
- Application, interviewing, and development planning

## Credential Milestones
- Pre-nursing: prerequisites, entrance examination, and program
  admission.
- Nursing program: clinical courses with competency validations,
  medication calculation examinations, and a capstone or
  preceptorship.
- Licensure: authorization to test, the NCLEX-RN, and state board
  licensure with background check; some jurisdictions participate in
  a multistate license compact.
- Practice: basic life support (and often advanced cardiac life
  support), employer competencies, a nurse residency where available,
  and specialty certification after the required practice hours.
- Advanced practice (nurse practitioner) requires a graduate degree,
  national certification, and state authorization.
- Requirements differ by country and state; confirm for your
  jurisdiction.

## Industry Relevance

Hospitals, Nurse Residency Programs, Long-Term Care, Community Health Organizations. Licensure and first employment are the decisive gates for new nurses, and employers select for demonstrated competency and readiness rather than grades alone. A readiness portfolio with measured exam preparation, a simulated assignment, and application materials is the definitive artifact for the nursing pathway.
