# User Research and Problem Definition

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Choosing a problem space and forming hypotheses before talking to users
2. Recruiting and interviewing users without leading them
3. Synthesizing interviews into themes, jobs, and pain points with evidence
4. Sizing the problem with available data
5. Writing a problem statement the team can align on

## What You'll Build
A research package on a real problem space for a product you can access
(an open-source tool, a product you use at work or school, or an app
with a community): a research plan with hypotheses, eight completed
user interviews with notes, a synthesis with themes and evidence,
quantitative sizing from any available data, and a one-page problem
statement with the opportunity, the affected users, and what success
would look like.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Eight real interviews is the floor; recruit from communities, forums,
  classmates, or colleagues who actually use the product.
- Do not propose solutions in this template; the discipline of staying
  in the problem is the exercise.
- Quantitative sizing can come from public usage data, forum volume, or
  a short survey.

## Steps
1. Choose the problem space and write three hypotheses about who is
   affected and why, stated so they can be wrong.
2. Write the interview guide: open questions about current behavior,
   recent examples, workarounds, and consequences, with no mention of
   solutions.
3. Recruit and run eight interviews, taking near-verbatim notes and
   recording where permitted.
4. Code the notes: tag every statement by theme, and build an affinity
   map of the themes with supporting quotes.
5. Identify the jobs users are trying to do, where the current
   experience fails, and how often and how badly, with evidence counts.
6. Size the problem: how many users are affected, how frequently, and
   what it costs them, from data or a short survey.
7. Write the problem statement: the user, the situation, the pain, the
   evidence, the opportunity, and measurable success criteria.
8. Present the findings to a peer or mentor playing the engineering
   lead, capture challenges to the evidence, and revise.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a competitive teardown of how others address the problem.
- Add a survey to validate the top theme at larger scale.
- Build personas grounded in the interview data.
- Run a follow-up round focused on the top theme.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Hypothesis-driven research planning
- Non-leading user interviewing and synthesis
- Evidence-based problem sizing
- Problem statements that align teams

## Industry Relevance

SaaS, Consumer Apps, Developer Tools. Product managers in these sectors are judged first on whether they understand users better than anyone in the room, and interview loops include research and problem-framing exercises. A research package with real interviews and a crisp problem statement is the foundation of a product portfolio.
