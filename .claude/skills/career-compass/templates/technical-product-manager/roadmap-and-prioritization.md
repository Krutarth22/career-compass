# Roadmap Prioritization with Trade-offs

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Gathering candidate initiatives from research, data, sales, support,
   and engineering
2. Estimating value and cost consistently and honestly
3. Making trade-offs explicit, including technical debt and platform work
4. Communicating a roadmap with confidence levels and what is not on it
5. Revisiting when new information arrives

## What You'll Build
A quarterly roadmap for a product you know (the one from your research,
or an open-source project with a public backlog): an initiative
inventory with sources, a scoring model with value and effort estimates
gathered from engineers, an explicit allocation across new features,
improvements, technical debt, and platform work, a roadmap document
with confidence levels and explicit cuts, a stakeholder review with
recorded objections, and a revision after a simulated change in
circumstances.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Open-source projects with active issue trackers are ideal: the
  backlog, maintainers, and users are real.
- Estimates should come from conversations with engineers or
  maintainers, not from you alone.
- The stakeholder review needs at least two people with different
  priorities (for example a maintainer and a heavy user).

## Steps
1. Build the initiative inventory from at least four sources (research,
   analytics, support or issues, engineering proposals) with the
   evidence for each.
2. Define the scoring model (reach, impact, confidence, effort, or an
   equivalent) and the allocation policy across work types.
3. Gather effort estimates and technical risks from engineers for each
   initiative, recording the conversations.
4. Score the initiatives, apply the allocation policy, and produce the
   ranked list with the cut line.
5. Write the roadmap document: themes, the initiatives per theme with
   confidence levels, what was cut and why, dependencies, and risks.
6. Run the stakeholder review, record every objection, and decide
   whether each changes the roadmap, documenting the reasoning.
7. Simulate a change (a competitor launch, a key engineer leaving, a
   large customer request) and revise the roadmap, showing what moved.
8. Write a short note on the trade-offs you found hardest and how the
   framework helped or failed.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a capacity model with team velocity and hiring plans.
- Publish a public roadmap for the open-source project with maintainer
  agreement.
- Add an opportunity-solution tree connecting outcomes to initiatives.
- Run the prioritization with two different frameworks and compare.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Initiative discovery from multiple sources
- Consistent value and effort estimation with engineers
- Explicit allocation and trade-off communication
- Stakeholder review and roadmap revision

## Industry Relevance

SaaS, Platform Companies, Enterprise Software. Roadmap ownership is the core accountability of product managers in these sectors, and interviews probe how you decide what not to build and how you handle pushback. A scored roadmap with recorded objections and a revision under changed circumstances demonstrates that judgment.
