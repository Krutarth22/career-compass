# Medical Device Development Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Developing a device from clinical need through verified prototype
2. Integrating hardware, software, and human factors under design
   controls
3. Managing risk, usability, and biocompatibility files together
4. Planning the regulatory pathway and testing strategy
5. Presenting the device at a design review with clinical input

## What You'll Build
A complete medical device development project of your own definition
(a physiological monitoring device, a rehabilitation aid with sensing,
a diagnostic sample-handling tool with software) carried through
design controls: clinical need and user needs from clinician
interviews, design inputs, architecture integrating sensing hardware,
software, and mechanical design, a risk management file, a usability
engineering file, a biocompatibility assessment, verification and
validation with reports, a regulatory strategy and testing plan, a
design history file, and a final design review with a clinician.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Reuse and extend the earlier templates' work; the capstone
  integrates them into one device with a complete file.
- Involve at least one clinician in needs gathering and the final
  review.
- Keep the device non-invasive and low risk so testing is safe.

## Steps
1. Gather the clinical need and user needs with clinician interviews
   and write the design inputs with acceptance criteria.
2. Design the system architecture and produce outputs for hardware,
   software (with lifecycle documentation), and mechanical parts.
3. Build the risk management file across the whole system, with
   controls implemented in the design.
4. Build and integrate the prototype, and run verification against
   every input with a report.
5. Conduct formative and summative usability evaluations and complete
   the usability file.
6. Complete the biocompatibility assessment and the regulatory
   strategy with classification, pathway, and required testing.
7. Validate with representative users in simulated use and write the
   validation report.
8. Assemble the design history file, hold the final design review with
   the clinician, and produce the portfolio package with a demo video.

## Extension Ideas
- Draft the premarket submission outline for the device.
- Run a small pilot study protocol through an ethics review.
- Perform a design transfer package for a contract manufacturer.
- Publish a technical description as a preprint or conference paper.

## Skills Demonstrated
- End-to-end medical device development under design controls
- Multidisciplinary integration of hardware, software, and human
  factors
- Complete risk, usability, and biocompatibility documentation
- Regulatory planning and clinician-involved review

## Industry Relevance

Medical Device Companies, Digital Health Hardware, Academic Medical Innovation, Contract Design and Development. Device development roles in these sectors require exactly this integrated, documented experience, and interviewers ask candidates to walk through a device they carried through design controls. A complete device with a full design history file is the definitive biomedical engineering portfolio.
