# Customer Discovery and Engagement Scoping

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Running discovery sessions with business and technical stakeholders
2. Mapping the customer's workflow, systems, and data as they actually are
3. Turning vague goals into a scoped statement of work with success metrics
4. Identifying risks (data access, security review, unclear owners) early
5. Getting written agreement before building anything

## What You'll Build
A complete discovery package for a realistic customer engagement you
simulate with a real organization you have access to (a student club, a
small business, a nonprofit, a team at your employer): interview notes
from at least three stakeholders, a current-state workflow and systems
map, a data inventory, a scoped statement of work with measurable
success criteria and explicit exclusions, a risk register, and a
kickoff deck the customer signs off on.

## Student-Scope Notes
- The "customer" must be a real group of people with a real problem,
  even if small. Inventing a customer removes the whole point.
- The deliverable is documents and a signed-off scope, not software.
  Building starts in the next templates.
- Keep the engagement to something achievable in four to six weeks of
  part-time work, since later templates deliver it.

## Steps
1. Identify the customer and the sponsor, and agree on a problem area
   worth an engagement (a manual process, a reporting gap, a workflow
   that loses data).
2. Prepare a discovery guide: questions on goals, current workflow,
   systems, data, who does what, what has been tried, and what success
   would look like in numbers.
3. Interview at least three stakeholders across roles (a user, a
   manager, someone who owns the systems), recording notes verbatim
   where possible.
4. Draw the current-state map: the workflow step by step, the systems
   and spreadsheets involved, and where data enters, moves, and gets
   lost.
5. Build the data inventory: every source, its owner, format, access
   method, sensitivity, and whether you have been granted access yet.
6. Write the statement of work: objectives, success metrics with
   baseline numbers, in-scope deliverables, explicit out-of-scope items,
   timeline with milestones, and what the customer must provide.
7. Write the risk register (access delays, security review, unclear
   ownership, data quality) with a mitigation and owner for each.
8. Present the kickoff deck to the sponsor, capture their changes, and
   obtain written sign-off. Write a short reflection on what discovery
   revealed that you had assumed wrongly.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a stakeholder map with influence and interest ratings.
- Add a lightweight value estimate (hours saved, revenue protected).
- Run a second discovery pass after two weeks and diff the findings.
- Draft a mutual action plan with dated commitments on both sides.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Structured stakeholder discovery and interviewing
- Current-state process and systems mapping
- Scoping with measurable success criteria and exclusions
- Risk identification and written customer alignment

## Industry Relevance

Enterprise AI Vendors, Data Platforms, Government and Defense Contractors. Forward-deployed engineers at companies in these sectors spend their first weeks on site doing exactly this, and the engagements that fail usually failed at scoping. A signed-off discovery package from a real customer is the strongest evidence you can do the non-coding half of the job.
