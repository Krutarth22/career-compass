# Fixture Design, Build, and Validation

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Defining fixture requirements from the process and the part's datums
2. Designing locating, clamping, and error-proofing features
3. Analyzing tolerance and clamping force
4. Building and validating the fixture with repeatability data
5. Documenting the fixture for maintenance and duplication

## What You'll Build
A fixture for one operation on your part (machining, assembly,
inspection, or soldering): a requirements list with datum scheme,
cycle-time target, and error-proofing needs, a design using the 3-2-1
locating principle with clamping analysis, a tolerance analysis from
part datums to the operation's critical dimension, a built fixture
(printed, machined, or assembled from modular components), a
repeatability study loading the part ten times, and a fixture drawing
package with a maintenance note.

## Student-Scope Notes
- 3D-printed fixtures with metal inserts and off-the-shelf clamps are
  legitimate for light-duty operations.
- Repeatability is measured with an indicator or calipers on a
  reference feature after each load.
- Include at least one poka-yoke feature that prevents wrong loading.

## Steps
1. Write the fixture requirements: the operation, the part's datum
   scheme, the critical dimensions affected, forces during the
   operation, load and unload time targets, and error-proofing.
2. Design the locating scheme with the 3-2-1 principle, choosing
   locator types and positions on the part's datums.
3. Design clamping: positions, directions, and forces calculated
   against the operation's loads, avoiding part distortion.
4. Add poka-yoke features so the part cannot be loaded wrong, and
   ergonomic features for loading.
5. Perform a tolerance analysis from locators through the fixture to
   the resulting dimension, and set fixture tolerances.
6. Build the fixture and measure the locator positions against the
   design.
7. Run the repeatability study: load and unload ten times, measuring
   the reference feature each time; compute the range and compare to
   the tolerance budget.
8. Produce the fixture drawings, a bill of materials, and a
   maintenance and inspection note, and write up the design and
   validation.

## Extension Ideas
- Add pneumatic clamping and a sequence valve.
- Design a fixture for the inspection of the part on a CMM.
- Run a small production batch with the fixture and chart results.
- Design a quick-change base for multiple part fixtures.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Fixture requirements from process and datum schemes
- Locating and clamping design with analysis
- Tolerance analysis for tooling
- Build validation and repeatability study

## Industry Relevance

Automotive Suppliers, Aerospace Manufacturing, Contract Manufacturers, Electronics Assembly. Fixture design is a daily responsibility of manufacturing engineers in these sectors, and repeatable tooling underpins every capability study. A validated fixture with a repeatability study and drawings is a concrete, respected work sample.
