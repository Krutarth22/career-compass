# Clinical Research Assistant Project

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Joining a clinical study team with the required training
2. Contributing to data collection, abstraction, or recruitment
3. Analyzing a defined question with supervision
4. Writing an abstract and presenting at a research forum
5. Building toward authorship through sustained contribution

## What You'll Build
A contribution to a real clinical research study as a research
assistant (through a lab, a department, or a student research
program): completed human subjects and GCP training certificates, a
defined role with documented tasks (chart abstraction, recruitment,
data entry, literature review), a supervised analysis of a specific
question from the study data or a related public dataset, an abstract
submitted to a student research day or conference, a poster or
presentation, and a mentor's evaluation.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Finding a position requires outreach; many schools have research
  matching programs.
- Your analysis question must be agreed with the principal
  investigator; do not analyze data outside your approved role.
- If no position is available, complete the training and analyze a
  public clinical dataset with a mentor's guidance, stated clearly.

## Steps
1. Complete human subjects protection and GCP training and file the
   certificates.
2. Secure a research position through outreach and agree on your role
   and time commitment in writing.
3. Perform your assigned tasks reliably for at least two months,
   keeping a log of contributions.
4. Propose a specific analytic question to the investigator and write
   a short analysis plan.
5. Conduct the analysis with supervision, with appropriate statistics
   and honest reporting.
6. Write an abstract to a conference's format and submit it to a
   student research day or a regional meeting.
7. Prepare and present the poster or talk, recording questions.
8. Request the mentor's written evaluation and discuss authorship
   expectations for any resulting publication.

## Extension Ideas
- Contribute to a manuscript as a co-author.
- Write a literature review section for the study.
- Learn the study's data management system and improve a process.
- Present at a national student research meeting.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Research ethics training and study team participation
- Reliable research task execution
- Supervised clinical data analysis
- Abstract writing and research presentation

## Credential Milestones
- Human subjects and GCP training certificates are required before
  any study involvement and are reportable on applications.
- Pre-medical applicants: research experience with a presentation is
  a recognized application component.
- Medical students: research output (abstracts, presentations,
  publications) is reported in ERAS and weighed by many residency
  programs, especially in competitive specialties.

## Industry Relevance

Academic Medical Centers, Teaching Hospitals, Clinical Research Departments, Residency Programs. Research participation with a presented abstract is a standard expectation for competitive applicants at every stage of medical training. A documented contribution with a mentor's evaluation and a presentation is the recognized evidence.
