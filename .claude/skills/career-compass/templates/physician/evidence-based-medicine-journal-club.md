# Evidence-Based Medicine Journal Club Series

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Framing a clinical question in a structured format
2. Searching for the best available evidence
3. Appraising a trial for validity, results, and applicability
4. Presenting the appraisal to peers and defending conclusions
5. Translating evidence into a recommendation for a patient scenario

## What You'll Build
A five-session journal club you organize and lead with peers: for each
session a clinical question in PICO format from a real or simulated
patient scenario, a documented search, a randomized trial or
systematic review appraised with a standard tool, a presentation
with the key statistics explained (effect sizes, confidence intervals,
number needed to treat), a bottom-line recommendation, session notes
with peer questions, and a written appraisal for each paper.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Recruit at least three peers; a journal club with an audience is the
  point.
- Use published appraisal tools and worksheets and cite them.
- Compute the key statistics yourself from the paper's data.

## Steps
1. Write five clinical questions in PICO format from scenarios across
   different specialties.
2. Search for the best evidence for each with documented strategy and
   select one primary paper per question.
3. Appraise each paper with the tool: randomization, allocation
   concealment, blinding, follow-up, intention-to-treat, and the
   results' precision.
4. Compute absolute and relative effects, confidence intervals, and
   number needed to treat or harm from the paper's data.
5. Assess applicability to the scenario's patient: similarity,
   outcomes that matter, values, and costs.
6. Prepare a ten-minute presentation and lead the session, inviting
   challenge and recording questions.
7. Write the bottom-line recommendation for each scenario with the
   strength of evidence.
8. Compile the series with the appraisals, presentations, and a
   reflection on how your reading of evidence changed.

## Extension Ideas
- Appraise a diagnostic accuracy study and a prognosis study.
- Present at a hospital or department journal club.
- Write a critically appraised topic summary for a repository.
- Run a session on a guideline and its evidence base.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Clinical question formulation and evidence searching
- Trial appraisal and statistical interpretation
- Presenting and defending evidence appraisal
- Evidence-based recommendation writing

## Credential Milestones
- Critical reading and reasoning skills feed directly into MCAT
  preparation (critical analysis and reasoning, biological and
  psychological foundations); register and schedule the exam within
  the year before application.
- Medical students: journal club participation is expected during
  clerkships and supports USMLE Step 1 and Step 2 biostatistics and
  epidemiology content.
- Maintain a list of appraisals for residency applications, where
  scholarly activity is reported.

## Industry Relevance

Academic Medical Centers, Teaching Hospitals, Residency Programs, Clinical Guideline Bodies. Evidence appraisal is a core competency assessed throughout medical training and board examinations, and residency programs value applicants who can lead a journal club. A documented series with computed statistics and recommendations shows that competency early.
