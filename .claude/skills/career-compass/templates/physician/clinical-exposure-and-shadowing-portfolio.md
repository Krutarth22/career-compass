# Clinical Exposure and Shadowing Portfolio

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Securing shadowing and volunteer placements through professional
   outreach
2. Observing clinical encounters with attention to reasoning and
   communication
3. Reflecting on patient experiences and system factors
4. Logging hours and experiences in the format applications require
5. Building relationships that lead to letters of recommendation

## What You'll Build
A documented clinical exposure portfolio: at least sixty hours across
two or more settings (a primary care clinic, an emergency department,
a hospice, a free clinic) including some direct patient contact
through volunteering or a certified role, a structured reflection
journal with entries after every session, an experience log in the
application format with hours and supervisor contacts, three written
reflections on specific encounters addressing ethics or social
determinants, and a summary of what the exposure confirmed or changed
about your goals.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Patient privacy is absolute: reflections must contain no identifying
  details.
- Shadowing alone is passive; pair it with a role involving direct
  contact (volunteer, scribe, EMT, CNA) where possible.
- Ask each supervisor early whether they would be willing to write a
  letter later, and keep contact details.

## Steps
1. Identify target settings and write outreach messages to clinicians
   and volunteer coordinators; track responses.
2. Complete any required onboarding (immunizations, privacy training,
   background checks) and log it.
3. Attend sessions and write a reflection within a day of each: what
   you observed, what the clinician reasoned, how communication
   worked, and what you felt.
4. Seek at least one placement with direct patient interaction and
   compare it with observation-only experiences.
5. Write three longer reflections on encounters that raised ethical
   questions, communication challenges, or social determinants of
   health.
6. Maintain the experience log with dates, hours, roles, and supervisor
   contacts in the format the application service uses.
7. Ask supervisors for feedback on your professionalism and record it.
8. Write the summary of insights and how they shape your motivation
   for medicine, in a form usable in a personal statement.

## Extension Ideas
- Obtain a certification (EMT, CNA, medical scribe) to deepen direct
  patient care.
- Volunteer longitudinally at one site for a year.
- Interview a physician about their career path and write it up.
- Shadow in a rural or underserved setting and compare.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Professional outreach and clinical onboarding
- Observational learning of clinical reasoning and communication
- Reflective practice with attention to ethics and equity
- Application-ready experience documentation

## Credential Milestones
- Pre-medical prerequisite coursework in progress or complete (biology,
  chemistry, organic chemistry, biochemistry, physics, mathematics,
  English, and often psychology and sociology); verify each target
  school's list.
- Clinical experience and shadowing hours accumulating; most
  competitive applicants exceed several hundred hours of combined
  clinical and volunteer experience.
- Begin MCAT preparation planning; the exam is typically taken in the
  year before application.
- Requirements vary by country and by school; confirm the specific
  pathway for your jurisdiction.

## Industry Relevance

Medical Schools, Hospitals, Community Clinics, Hospice Organizations. Admissions committees weigh sustained clinical exposure and reflective maturity heavily, and letters from clinicians who observed you matter. A portfolio with logged hours, reflections, and supervisor relationships is the foundation of a competitive application.
