# Physician Pathway Readiness Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Assembling a complete application or training-stage portfolio
2. Preparing for high-stakes examinations with a measured plan
3. Practicing interviews and clinical scenarios with feedback
4. Demonstrating competencies with evidence across domains
5. Planning the next stage with milestones and contingencies

## What You'll Build
A readiness portfolio for your next stage (medical school application,
clerkships, or residency application): the application materials
(personal statement, experience descriptions, letters requested and
tracked), an examination preparation plan with baseline and progress
data from practice tests, a competency evidence map linking your
portfolio items (clinical exposure, journal club, case report, QI
project, research) to the competencies the next stage assesses, at
least three mock interviews or structured clinical scenarios with
feedback, an ethics case analysis, and a milestone plan with
contingencies for the next two years.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Tailor to your actual stage; the structure is the same whether the
  next step is admission, clerkships, or the Match.
- Mock interviews should use the format your target uses (traditional,
  multiple mini interview, or residency panel).
- Practice-test data must be real; the plan is judged on measurement,
  not optimism.

## Steps
1. Identify the next stage and its requirements, and build the
   competency evidence map from your portfolio.
2. Draft the personal statement and experience descriptions, and
   obtain feedback from at least two reviewers, one a clinician.
3. Request letters of recommendation with a packet for each writer
   and track them.
4. Take a baseline practice examination, build the study plan with
   weekly targets, and track scores over at least eight weeks.
5. Complete three mock interviews or structured clinical scenarios
   with feedback, and revise your approach each time.
6. Write an ethics case analysis on a scenario relevant to the next
   stage using a structured framework.
7. Assemble the portfolio with the evidence map, materials, exam data,
   interview feedback, and the ethics analysis.
8. Write the two-year milestone plan with dates, decision points, and
   contingencies, and review it with a mentor.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a specialty exploration analysis with shadowing in three fields.
- Build a longitudinal learning portfolio for use through training.
- Prepare a teaching session for junior students on one competency.
- Present the portfolio to an advisor for a formal readiness review.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Application and portfolio assembly with competency mapping
- Measured examination preparation
- Interview and scenario performance under feedback
- Stage planning with contingencies

## Credential Milestones
- Pre-medical: prerequisites complete, MCAT taken, primary application
  submitted through the application service, secondary applications,
  interviews, and acceptance.
- Medical school: preclinical curriculum, USMLE Step 1 (or COMLEX
  Level 1), core clerkships, USMLE Step 2 (or COMLEX Level 2), and
  sub-internships.
- Residency: ERAS application, interviews, the Match, then
  postgraduate training with in-training examinations and USMLE Step 3
  (or COMLEX Level 3) for licensure.
- Practice: state medical licensure, board certification in the
  specialty, and ongoing maintenance of certification and continuing
  medical education.
- Pathways differ by country; confirm the sequence for your
  jurisdiction.

## Industry Relevance

Medical Schools, Residency Programs, Teaching Hospitals, Licensing Boards. Progression in medicine is a series of high-stakes gates, each judged on evidence of competencies and readiness, and structured preparation measurably improves outcomes at each. A complete readiness portfolio with a milestone plan is the definitive artifact for the physician pathway.
