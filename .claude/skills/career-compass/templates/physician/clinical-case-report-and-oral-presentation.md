# Clinical Case Report and Oral Presentation

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Identifying a teaching case with a supervising clinician
2. Obtaining consent and de-identifying appropriately
3. Writing the case with a structured reasoning narrative and
   literature context
4. Delivering a formal oral case presentation
5. Submitting to a student conference or journal

## What You'll Build
A case report written with a supervising clinician following the CARE
guidelines (or an equivalent structure using a published teaching case
if you lack clinical access): patient consent documentation or a
documented teaching-case source, a de-identified case narrative with
timeline, the clinical reasoning including the differential and how
it narrowed, a focused literature review situating the case, a
discussion of learning points, a formal oral presentation delivered
in the standard format and recorded, and a submission to a student
conference, poster session, or case report journal.

## Student-Scope Notes
- A supervising clinician must be involved for a real case; consent
  and de-identification follow institutional rules.
- Without clinical access, use a published open-access case and write
  a structured teaching analysis of it, stated clearly.
- The oral presentation must follow the standard order (chief
  complaint, history, exam, data, assessment, plan) and stay under
  seven minutes.

## Steps
1. Identify the case with the clinician and confirm its teaching
   value; obtain written consent from the patient or document the
   published source.
2. Assemble the timeline of presentation, findings, investigations,
   interventions, and outcomes, de-identified.
3. Write the clinical reasoning narrative: the initial differential,
   the discriminating features, and the diagnostic pathway.
4. Search the literature for similar cases and relevant guidelines and
   write the discussion situating the case.
5. Draft the report to the guideline structure and revise with the
   clinician's feedback.
6. Prepare the oral presentation and rehearse to time; deliver it to
   peers or at a morning report and record it.
7. Handle questions and incorporate feedback into the written report.
8. Submit to a student conference, poster session, or journal and
   document the submission and outcome.

## Extension Ideas
- Turn the case into a teaching vignette with questions for peers.
- Present at a regional student research day.
- Write a second report on a management dilemma rather than a
  diagnosis.
- Develop a clinical reasoning concept map from the case.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Case selection, consent, and de-identification
- Clinical reasoning articulated in writing
- Literature contextualization of a case
- Formal oral case presentation

## Credential Milestones
- Medical students: case presentations are assessed on every clinical
  clerkship; the oral presentation format is the same one used on
  rounds and in the USMLE Step 2 clinical reasoning content.
- Scholarly output (a case report accepted at a conference or journal)
  strengthens residency applications through ERAS.
- Pre-medical applicants using a published case: the written analysis
  demonstrates reasoning and communication for interviews.

## Industry Relevance

Teaching Hospitals, Residency Programs, Medical Journals, Academic Medical Centers. Case presentation is the daily currency of clinical teaching, and a published or presented case report is a recognized scholarly credential in residency selection. A completed report with a recorded presentation demonstrates both the reasoning and the communication the profession expects.
