# Clinical Quality Improvement PDSA Project

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Identifying an improvement opportunity with a clinical team
2. Defining an aim, measures, and a change idea
3. Running plan-do-study-act cycles with data
4. Engaging the interprofessional team throughout
5. Reporting results in the standard improvement format

## What You'll Build
A quality improvement project in a clinical or community health
setting where you volunteer or train (vaccination reminders at a free
clinic, wait times, discharge instruction clarity, hand hygiene
compliance): a charter with a SMART aim, a process map and cause
analysis, outcome, process, and balancing measures, at least two
completed PDSA cycles with run charts, team engagement documented,
and a report following SQUIRE guidelines with a poster.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Requires a setting that agrees to host a small project; free
  clinics and student-run clinics are common hosts.
- Determine with the site whether ethics review is needed; most
  improvement projects are exempt but this must be documented.
- Two cycles with real data is the floor; the change need not succeed.

## Steps
1. Meet the team, identify a problem they care about, and write the
   charter with a SMART aim and scope.
2. Map the current process and analyze causes with a fishbone diagram
   and the team's input.
3. Define the measures and establish a baseline with at least two
   weeks of data.
4. Select a change idea, plan the first PDSA cycle with a prediction,
   and run it.
5. Study the data on a run chart, decide to adapt, adopt, or abandon,
   and run the second cycle.
6. Engage the interprofessional team at each cycle with brief
   huddles and record their input.
7. Analyze results with run chart rules and assess the balancing
   measures.
8. Write the SQUIRE-format report and a poster, present to the team,
   and hand over a sustainment plan.

## Extension Ideas
- Run a third cycle and a spread plan to another site.
- Submit the poster to a quality improvement conference.
- Add a cost analysis of the improvement.
- Complete a recognized improvement training certificate.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Improvement project chartering with clinical teams
- Measurement, run charts, and PDSA cycles
- Interprofessional engagement
- Improvement reporting to a published standard

## Credential Milestones
- Medical students: quality improvement participation is an
  accreditation-required competency and reportable scholarly activity
  for residency applications.
- Residents: improvement projects are required in training and count
  toward board-certification practice improvement components.
- Pre-medical applicants: a completed project with a team demonstrates
  leadership and systems thinking valued in admissions.

## Industry Relevance

Hospitals, Health Systems, Residency Programs, Community Clinics. Quality improvement is a required competency across medical training and a leadership pathway in practice, and residency programs ask applicants about improvement work specifically. A completed project with cycles, data, and a report demonstrates that competency with evidence.
