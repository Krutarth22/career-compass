# Technical Discovery and Deal Qualification

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Running a discovery call that uncovers the technical situation and the
   business driver
2. Mapping the prospect's architecture and integration points
3. Qualifying fit honestly, including when the product is wrong for them
4. Writing the discovery summary that drives the demo and the proof of
   concept
5. Aligning with the account executive on next steps and risks

## What You'll Build
A discovery toolkit and three completed discovery write-ups: a question
bank organized by theme, a call plan template, and write-ups from three
real conversations (with peers, local businesses, open-source project
users, or contacts who agree to role-play from a real situation) each
with an architecture sketch, a fit assessment with a recommendation, and
proposed next steps.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Real conversations with people describing a real situation are
  required, even if the "deal" is hypothetical.
- Pick one product to qualify for across all three conversations so the
  fit assessments are comparable.
- Include at least one conversation where the honest answer is that the
  product is not a fit.

## Steps
1. Write the question bank: business driver, current state, technical
   environment, integration points, security and compliance needs,
   decision process, timeline, and success criteria.
2. Write the call plan template: goals, attendees, questions to
   prioritize, and the summary you will send afterward.
3. Run the first discovery conversation, taking notes, and within a day
   write the summary: situation, pain, architecture sketch, requirements,
   open questions.
4. Assess fit against the product's actual capabilities, with a clear
   recommendation and the risks or gaps.
5. Run the second and third conversations, refining the question bank
   after each based on what you missed.
6. For each, write the internal handoff note an account executive would
   need: fit, risks, champion, decision process, and recommended next
   step (demo, proof of concept, or disqualify).
7. Draft the follow-up email to each prospect that reflects their words
   back and proposes the next step.
8. Write up what changed in your question bank across the three calls
   and the pattern of what prospects did not volunteer until asked.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a qualification scorecard (MEDDIC or similar) and score each.
- Record one call (with permission) and review it for talk ratio.
- Build an architecture diagram template for common integration patterns.
- Practice the "not a fit" conversation and document how it landed.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Structured technical discovery and active listening
- Architecture mapping from conversation
- Honest fit assessment and qualification
- Internal handoff and customer follow-up writing

## Industry Relevance

B2B SaaS, Data Infrastructure, Enterprise Security. Solutions engineering teams in these sectors measure discovery quality because it predicts win rate and prevents wasted proofs of concept, and interviews often include a mock discovery call. A question bank refined over real conversations, plus honest fit write-ups, is direct preparation.
