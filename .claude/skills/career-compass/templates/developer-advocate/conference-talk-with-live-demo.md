# Conference Talk with a Live Demo

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Writing a talk abstract that gets accepted by a program committee
2. Structuring a technical talk around a narrative and one clear takeaway
3. Building a live demo that survives bad wifi and nerves
4. Rehearsing to time and handling questions
5. Delivering, recording, and repurposing the talk

## What You'll Build
A twenty-five-minute technical talk on a topic from your tutorial
series, submitted to at least three meetups or conferences (with the
abstracts and outcomes recorded), delivered at least once to a real
audience (a meetup, a user group, an internal tech talk, or a recorded
livestream with viewers), with a live demo that has a rehearsed
fallback, slides, a recording, and a written version published
afterward.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Meetups and internal talks count. The audience must be real and not
  only friends.
- The demo must run from a fresh state on stage; a pre-recorded fallback
  video is required, not optional.
- Rehearse with a timer at least five times, twice in front of someone.

## Steps
1. Choose the topic and write the one-sentence takeaway the audience
   should leave with. Draft three abstract variants and pick the
   strongest with peer feedback.
2. Submit to at least three calls for proposals or meetup organizers and
   record the outcomes.
3. Outline the talk: hook, the problem, the concept, the demo, the
   takeaway, and a call to action, with time budgets per section.
4. Build the demo from a clean state with a scripted setup, minimal
   dependencies on network, large fonts, and a fallback video of the
   full run.
5. Build the slides: sparse, visual, with code shown in small,
   readable chunks.
6. Rehearse five times to time, including once with the demo
   deliberately failing and the fallback used, and twice with a peer
   giving feedback.
7. Deliver the talk, record it, and collect audience questions and
   feedback.
8. Publish the recording and a written version, and write a
   retrospective on what landed, what did not, and the abstract
   outcomes.

## Extension Ideas
- Deliver the talk a second time and compare the two recordings.
- Turn the talk into a workshop with hands-on exercises.
- Submit to a larger conference with a revised abstract.
- Produce a five-minute lightning version.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Abstract writing and call-for-proposals submission
- Technical narrative structure and slide design
- Resilient live demos with rehearsed fallbacks
- Delivery, recording, and content repurposing

## Industry Relevance

Developer Tools, Cloud Platforms, Open-Source Companies. Speaking is a core channel for developer relations in these sectors, and hiring managers ask for a recorded talk and evidence you can get accepted and deliver reliably. A recorded talk with a live demo and submission history is the standard portfolio requirement.
