# Video Tutorials and a Live Coding Stream

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Scripting short technical videos that respect viewer time
2. Recording clean screencasts with readable code and clear audio
3. Editing for pace and publishing with searchable titles and chapters
4. Running a live coding session with audience interaction
5. Measuring retention and iterating on format

## What You'll Build
Three short video tutorials (five to ten minutes each) adapted from your
written tutorial series, produced with scripts, clean screencasts,
captions, and chapters, plus one live coding stream of at least forty-five
minutes building something real with audience questions, published with
analytics, and a review of retention data that informs a revised fourth
video.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Free tools are sufficient: OBS for capture and streaming, any basic
  editor, auto-captions corrected by hand.
- Audio quality matters more than video quality; a decent microphone
  in a quiet room is the main requirement.
- The stream needs real viewers; announce it in the community you have
  been participating in.

## Steps
1. Script the first video from a tutorial: one goal, the minimal steps,
   what appears on screen, and the narration, targeting under eight
   minutes.
2. Set up recording: large fonts, high-contrast theme, a clean desktop,
   a test recording checked for audio levels and readability.
3. Record and edit the first video, cut dead time, add captions and
   chapters, and publish with a searchable title and description.
4. Produce the second and third videos, refining the process and
   template each time.
5. Plan the live stream: the project to build, the milestones, the
   moments to invite questions, and a fallback if something breaks.
6. Run the stream, engage with chat, finish the build, and publish the
   recording with chapters.
7. Review analytics after two weeks: retention curves, where viewers
   drop, click-through from search, and comments.
8. Produce a fourth video applying what the retention data showed, and
   write up the process, the metrics, and the format decisions.

## Extension Ideas
- Produce a short-form vertical cut of the best moments.
- Run a recurring stream series with a consistent slot.
- Add a companion repository with the stream's code and timestamps.
- Collaborate with another creator or maintainer on a stream.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Scripting and producing concise technical video
- Screencast and livestream production
- Publishing with captions, chapters, and searchable metadata
- Retention analysis and format iteration

## Industry Relevance

Developer Tools, Cloud Platforms, EdTech for Developers. Video and streaming are primary developer education channels in these sectors, and DevRel roles increasingly require production ability alongside writing and speaking. Published videos with retention data and a live stream recording demonstrate that range.
