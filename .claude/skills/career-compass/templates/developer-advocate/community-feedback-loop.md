# Community Feedback Loop to Product

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Being present where developers talk about the product and answering well
2. Capturing feedback systematically instead of anecdotally
3. Synthesizing themes with evidence and quantifying them
4. Delivering a regular, credible feedback report to product teams
5. Closing the loop back to the community when things change

## What You'll Build
A running feedback program for a developer product or open-source
project over six weeks: a presence in its community channels with a log
of your responses, a structured feedback capture system (tagged,
sourced, deduplicated), a synthesized report every two weeks with
themes, counts, representative quotes, and recommendations, and a
public "you said, we did" update for at least one change that resulted
or was already in progress.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Choose a project whose community you can genuinely participate in
  (forum, Discord, GitHub discussions, Stack Overflow tag).
- Answer questions to be helpful first; feedback capture rides on
  authentic participation.
- If you do not work for the vendor, deliver the report to maintainers
  publicly or through their preferred channel.

## Steps
1. Map the community channels, their norms, and who the maintainers or
   product owners are.
2. Set up the capture system: a spreadsheet or tracker with source,
   date, quote, category, product area, sentiment, and a link.
3. Participate daily for six weeks: answer questions, ask clarifying
   questions, and log every piece of feedback, with a minimum of fifty
   entries.
4. Every two weeks, deduplicate and tag the entries, count by theme,
   and pick representative quotes.
5. Write the feedback report: top themes with counts and trends,
   quotes, the developer impact, and specific recommendations ranked.
6. Deliver the report to the product owners or maintainers and record
   their responses and any decisions.
7. Publish a community update on at least one change that resulted or
   was already in progress, crediting the feedback.
8. Write up the program: response volume, feedback captured, themes
   over time, what changed, and how you would scale the process.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a lightweight survey to validate the top themes quantitatively.
- Build a public roadmap or changelog fed by the feedback themes.
- Set up an advisory group from the most engaged community members.
- Automate capture from GitHub issues with tagging.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Authentic community participation and support
- Systematic feedback capture and synthesis
- Evidence-based product reporting
- Closing the loop publicly

## Industry Relevance

Open-Source Companies, Developer Platforms, API Vendors. Developer relations in these sectors is valued for bringing structured community insight to product, and the credibility of that channel depends on rigor. Six weeks of logged participation with quantified reports and a closed loop demonstrates the discipline behind the role.
