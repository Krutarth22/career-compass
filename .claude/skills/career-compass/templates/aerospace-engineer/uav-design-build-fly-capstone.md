# UAV Design, Build, and Flight Test Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Running a full aircraft development cycle from requirements to
   flight test
2. Integrating aerodynamics, structures, propulsion, and control
3. Managing weight, balance, and systems integration
4. Planning and executing flight tests safely with instrumentation
5. Reporting performance against requirements and lessons learned

## What You'll Build
A small fixed-wing UAV designed, built, and flown by you: requirements
and a concept design with trades, the integrated aerodynamic,
structural, propulsion, and control designs from earlier templates,
detailed CAD and a build with weight and balance tracking, ground
tests (structural proof, propulsion, control surface, range check), a
flight test plan with safety and airspace compliance, instrumented
flights with a flight controller logging data, performance analysis
against the requirements, and a final report with a design review
record.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Comply with local regulations for model aircraft and drones; document
  the rules you followed and the location's approval.
- Keep the aircraft small (under the regulatory threshold for hobby
  flight) and use an off-the-shelf flight controller for logging and
  optionally stabilization.
- A safety pilot with experience for first flights is strongly
  recommended.

## Steps
1. Write the requirements (mission, performance, weight, cost, safety)
   and run a concept trade with at least two configurations; hold a
   concept review.
2. Integrate the wing, tail, spar, fuselage, propulsion, and control
   designs into a detailed CAD model with a weight and balance
   spreadsheet updated as parts are made.
3. Build the airframe, documenting the process and actual weights, and
   verify the center of gravity against the stability analysis.
4. Perform ground tests: structural proof load on the wing, propulsion
   static test installed, control surface deflection and direction
   checks, and a radio range check.
5. Write the flight test plan: test cards for each flight with
   objectives, procedures, limits, abort criteria, and safety roles.
6. Fly the test program incrementally (taxi, first flight, trim,
   performance, handling), logging flight data on every flight.
7. Analyze the data: cruise speed and power, climb rate, stall
   behavior, and handling, against predictions and requirements.
8. Write the final report with the design, build, test plan, data,
   requirement verification matrix, and lessons learned, and present
   it at a final review.

## Extension Ideas
- Enable the autopilot and verify your control gains in flight.
- Add a payload and demonstrate the mission.
- Perform a wind tunnel test of a wing section and compare.
- Iterate to a second aircraft using the lessons learned.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Full aircraft development cycle with reviews
- Multidisciplinary design integration and weight control
- Ground and flight test planning and execution
- Performance analysis and requirements verification

## Industry Relevance

UAV Manufacturers, Aircraft Manufacturers, Defense Contractors, Aerospace Research. A design-build-fly project is the closest a student can come to an aircraft development program, and hiring managers in these sectors treat it as the strongest evidence of integrated aerospace capability. A flown aircraft with data-backed verification is the definitive aerospace portfolio piece.
