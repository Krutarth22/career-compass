# Designed Biological Experiment with Statistical Analysis

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Formulating a testable hypothesis and pre-registering the analysis
2. Designing with adequate replication, randomization, and blinding
3. Executing with controls and recording deviations
4. Analyzing with appropriate statistics and effect sizes
5. Reporting transparently, including negative results

## What You'll Build
A hypothesis-driven experiment in your lab context (the effect of a
treatment on cell viability, growth conditions on bacterial yield,
a variable in a molecular protocol on outcome): a pre-registered
protocol with hypothesis, sample size justification from pilot
variability, randomization and blinding plans, execution with
biological and technical replicates, a pre-specified analysis in R
with effect sizes and intervals, diagnostic checks, a transparent
report including deviations, and a public repository of data and
code.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Pre-register the protocol (a timestamped document or a public
  registry) before collecting data.
- Biological replicates must be genuinely independent; explain how.
- Report the result whether or not it supports the hypothesis.

## Steps
1. Write the hypothesis, the primary outcome, and the analysis plan,
   and estimate the sample size from pilot or literature variability.
2. Design the experiment: treatment groups, controls, biological and
   technical replicates, randomization of order and layout, and
   blinding where possible.
3. Pre-register the protocol with a timestamp.
4. Execute the experiment with full notebook records, including every
   deviation.
5. Enter data with a validation step and store raw files immutably.
6. Run the pre-specified analysis: the model, assumption diagnostics,
   effect sizes with confidence intervals, and any pre-specified
   secondary analyses.
7. Produce figures showing individual data points, not just summaries,
   with clear legends.
8. Write the report with methods sufficient for replication, results,
   deviations, limitations, and a link to the data and code.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a second factor and analyze the interaction.
- Replicate the experiment independently and compare.
- Perform a Bayesian analysis alongside the frequentist one.
- Write it as a registered report for a journal format.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Hypothesis formulation and pre-registration
- Rigorous experimental design with replication and blinding
- Appropriate statistical analysis with effect sizes
- Transparent reporting and open data

## Industry Relevance

Academic Research, Pharmaceutical Research, Biotechnology, Contract Research Organizations. Reproducibility concerns have made rigorous design and pre-specified analysis a hiring criterion in these sectors, and scientists who report transparently are trusted. A pre-registered experiment with open data and an honest report demonstrates that rigor.
