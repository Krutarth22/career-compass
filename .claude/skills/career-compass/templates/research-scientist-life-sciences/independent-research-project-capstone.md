# Independent Research Project Capstone

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Owning a research question from proposal to presentation
2. Executing a multi-experiment plan with adaptation
3. Maintaining rigor, records, and safety throughout
4. Analyzing and integrating results into a coherent story
5. Communicating through a manuscript, a poster, and a talk

## What You'll Build
An independent research project executed over several months in a lab:
the proposal from your earlier template (or a new one) approved by a
supervisor, an experimental plan with milestones, execution of at
least three linked experiments using your validated assay and core
techniques, a complete notebook and data archive, integrated analysis
with pre-specified and exploratory components clearly separated, a
manuscript-style write-up, a conference-style poster, a recorded
fifteen-minute talk, and a supervisor's evaluation.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Requires a lab and a supervising scientist; volunteer or course-based
  research positions are the typical routes.
- Scope the question to what three linked experiments can address.
- Negative or ambiguous results written up honestly are fully
  acceptable outcomes.

## Steps
1. Finalize the proposal with the supervisor and write the
   experimental plan with milestones and decision points.
2. Execute the first experiment, analyze it, and decide with the
   supervisor how it shapes the second.
3. Execute the second and third experiments, adapting the plan as
   results warrant and recording the reasoning.
4. Maintain the notebook and data archive continuously, with safety
   and integrity practices, and have the supervisor review them.
5. Integrate the analysis: pre-specified tests, exploratory findings
   clearly labeled, and the figures.
6. Write the manuscript-style report with abstract, introduction,
   methods, results, discussion, and limitations.
7. Prepare the poster and present it to lab members or at a student
   symposium; record the talk and collect questions.
8. Obtain the supervisor's written evaluation, write a reflection on
   what you would do differently, and assemble the portfolio.

## Extension Ideas
- Submit the work as a preprint or to a student journal.
- Present at a regional conference.
- Train a newer student on your assay using your procedure.
- Write a follow-up proposal based on the results.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Independent research execution with supervision
- Adaptive multi-experiment planning
- Rigorous records and integrated analysis
- Scientific communication in three formats

## Industry Relevance

Academic Research, Biotechnology, Pharmaceutical Research, Graduate Programs. Research scientist and graduate admissions decisions in these sectors rest on evidence of independent research with real results and communication, and a supervisor's evaluation carries weight. A completed project with a manuscript, poster, and talk is the definitive life sciences research portfolio.
