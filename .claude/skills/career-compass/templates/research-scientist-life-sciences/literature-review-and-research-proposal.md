# Literature Review and Research Proposal

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Searching and organizing the literature on a focused question
2. Critically appraising key papers
3. Identifying a gap and framing specific aims
4. Designing experiments with feasibility, timeline, and budget
5. Writing to a funder's or a lab's proposal format

## What You'll Build
A research proposal on a focused biological question: a structured
literature search with a reference manager, a critical appraisal of
at least ten key papers with a consistent rubric, a narrative review
synthesizing the field and identifying the gap, specific aims with
hypotheses, an experimental design for each aim with controls,
expected results, pitfalls, and alternatives, a timeline, a budget
with justification, and a proposal in a standard format reviewed by
a scientist with a written critique and your response.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Choose a question narrow enough that ten papers cover its core.
- Use a real proposal format (a fellowship application or a
  foundation's format) and cite it.
- The reviewer should be a scientist who has written or reviewed
  proposals.

## Steps
1. Define the question and run a structured search with documented
   strings and a reference manager library.
2. Appraise the key papers with a rubric: question, design, controls,
   statistics, limitations, and contribution.
3. Write the narrative review synthesizing findings, contradictions,
   and the gap.
4. Frame two or three specific aims with testable hypotheses.
5. Design the experiments for each aim with controls, sample sizes,
   expected results, potential pitfalls, and alternative approaches.
6. Build the timeline with milestones and the budget with
   justification for personnel, reagents, and equipment.
7. Write the proposal in the chosen format with a specific aims page,
   background and significance, approach, and references.
8. Obtain the scientist's critique, write a point-by-point response,
   and revise.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a preliminary data section from your own experiments.
- Submit to a student fellowship or an internal funding competition.
- Present the proposal as a chalk talk and record it.
- Write a lay summary for a public audience.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Structured literature searching and critical appraisal
- Gap identification and aim framing
- Experimental design at the proposal level
- Scientific proposal writing and response to review

## Industry Relevance

Academic Research, Biotechnology R&D, Pharmaceutical Research, Scientific Funding Organizations. The ability to synthesize a field and propose experiments distinguishes scientists from technicians in these sectors, and it is required for graduate study, fellowships, and industry project leadership. A reviewed proposal with a response to critique demonstrates that scientific maturity.
