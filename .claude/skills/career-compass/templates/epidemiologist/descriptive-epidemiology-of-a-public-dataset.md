# Descriptive Epidemiology of a Public Health Dataset

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Acquiring surveillance or survey data and understanding its design
2. Computing rates with correct denominators and standardization
3. Describing person, place, and time patterns
4. Accounting for survey weights or reporting artifacts
5. Communicating findings in a public health report

## What You'll Build
A descriptive analysis of a public health dataset (a national health
survey, notifiable disease surveillance data, mortality data): a data
provenance and design summary, incidence or prevalence rates with
correct denominators, age-standardized rates for comparison across
groups or areas, trends over time with appropriate smoothing, a
person-place-time characterization with maps and charts, an assessment
of data limitations (reporting delays, changes in definitions), and a
public health brief.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use survey weights correctly where the data are from a complex
  survey; document the design.
- Population denominators must come from a cited source matching the
  numerator's geography and years.
- The brief should be two to four pages for a non-technical health
  official.

## Steps
1. Select the dataset and read its documentation: design, variables,
   coverage, and known limitations.
2. Obtain matching population denominators and document their source.
3. Compute crude rates by age, sex, and geography with confidence
   intervals.
4. Compute age-standardized rates with a stated standard population
   and compare groups or areas.
5. Analyze trends over time, addressing reporting delay or definition
   changes, and present with appropriate uncertainty.
6. Map geographic patterns with attention to small numbers and
   suppression rules.
7. Write the limitations section honestly: completeness, bias in
   reporting, and comparability.
8. Write the public health brief with key findings, figures, and
   implications, and the technical appendix with code.

## Extension Ideas
- Add an analysis of health disparities with an equity framing.
- Compare two data sources measuring the same condition.
- Add a forecast of the trend with uncertainty.
- Produce an interactive dashboard of the descriptive results.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Surveillance and survey data handling
- Rate computation and standardization
- Person-place-time analysis and visualization
- Public health reporting with limitations

## Industry Relevance

Public Health Departments, Government Health Agencies, Nonprofit Health Organizations, Academic Public Health. Descriptive epidemiology is the daily work of surveillance epidemiologists in these settings, and reports for officials must be correct about denominators and honest about limitations. A brief with standardized rates and a technical appendix is the standard entry portfolio piece.
