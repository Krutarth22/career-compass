# History and Physical Practice with SOAP Notes

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Taking a complete and a focused history with a structured approach
2. Performing a systematic physical examination
3. Documenting in SOAP format with an assessment and plan
4. Presenting the patient concisely
5. Receiving feedback and refining technique

## What You'll Build
A history and physical examination practice record: ten complete
encounters with peers or standardized patients using scripted cases
(five complete histories and physicals, five focused encounters for
common complaints), each documented as a SOAP note with a
differential diagnosis and plan, video-recorded where possible and
scored against a checklist by a peer or clinician, oral presentations
of three cases, and a progression summary showing improvement in
checklist scores and note quality.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Peers as patients with scripts is acceptable; a school's
  standardized patient program is better if accessible.
- Physical examination practice on peers is limited to
  non-invasive components; document the limits.
- Use a published checklist for history and examination components.

## Steps
1. Obtain or write ten case scripts with a chief complaint, history
   details, and examination findings to be simulated.
2. Learn the structured history framework and the head-to-toe
   examination sequence, and practice each component.
3. Conduct the five complete encounters, recording where possible, and
   write a full SOAP note with a differential for each.
4. Conduct the five focused encounters for common complaints (chest
   pain, abdominal pain, headache, cough, back pain) with focused
   examinations and notes.
5. Have each encounter scored against the checklist and each note
   reviewed for completeness and reasoning.
6. Present three cases orally to a peer or clinician in the standard
   format and receive feedback.
7. Remediate weak components with targeted practice and re-score at
   least two encounters.
8. Compile the record with scores over time, sample notes, and a
   reflection on reasoning development.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a pediatric and a geriatric encounter with adapted approaches.
- Practice a difficult conversation (delivering bad news) with a
  script.
- Add telehealth encounter practice.
- Complete an objective structured clinical examination style
  circuit.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Structured history taking
- Systematic physical examination
- SOAP documentation with differential reasoning
- Oral case presentation and feedback incorporation

## Credential Milestones
- Physician assistant students: history and physical examination
  competence is assessed through objective structured clinical
  examinations during the didactic year and required before clinical
  rotations.
- Pre-physician assistant applicants: this practice demonstrates
  readiness and supports interview performance in clinical scenario
  formats.
- Documentation skills map to the clinical content of the national
  certifying examination.

## Industry Relevance

Physician Assistant Programs, Clinics, Hospitals, Urgent Care. The history and physical examination with clear documentation is the core clinical skill of physician assistants across every setting. A practice record with scored encounters and reviewed notes demonstrates the skill that clinical preceptors evaluate from the first day of rotations.
