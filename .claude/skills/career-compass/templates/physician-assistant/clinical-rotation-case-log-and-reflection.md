# Clinical Rotation Case Log and Reflection

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Logging every patient encounter with diagnoses and procedures
2. Tracking exposure against required competencies
3. Identifying gaps and seeking experiences to fill them
4. Reflecting on cases weekly to consolidate learning
5. Preparing for rotation examinations from real cases

## What You'll Build
A rotation-style case log and learning system, applied to a real
clinical rotation if you are a student, or to your patient care role
and shadowing if pre-program: a structured log of at least one
hundred encounters with setting, chief complaint, diagnoses,
procedures observed or performed, and your role, a competency
coverage tracker against a published required-exposure list, a gap
analysis with actions taken, weekly reflections on the most
instructive cases, a set of self-authored practice questions from
real cases, and a rotation summary with preceptor feedback.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use your program's logging system if you are a student; build a
  spreadsheet otherwise.
- No identifying information in the log or reflections.
- One hundred encounters is the floor; students will exceed it.

## Steps
1. Build the log structure with the fields your program or the
   accreditation standards require.
2. Log every encounter promptly with diagnosis codes or categories and
   procedures.
3. Map the log against the required-exposure list weekly and identify
   gaps.
4. Act on gaps: request specific patient types or procedures from the
   preceptor and document the outcome.
5. Write a weekly reflection on the most instructive cases: what you
   learned, what you would do differently, and what to read.
6. Write practice questions from real cases in the examination style
   and use them for review.
7. Collect preceptor feedback at midpoint and end, and record your
   responses.
8. Write the rotation summary with exposure statistics, gaps closed,
   and learning consolidated.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a procedure skills log with supervised counts toward competence.
- Analyze your log for social determinants patterns in your patients.
- Present a rotation case at a program conference.
- Build a longitudinal log across all rotations.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Systematic encounter logging
- Competency exposure tracking and gap management
- Reflective learning from real cases
- Self-directed examination preparation

## Credential Milestones
- Physician assistant students: patient encounter logging against
  required exposures is an accreditation requirement; end-of-rotation
  examinations follow each rotation.
- Clinical year completion leads to the national certifying
  examination (PANCE); eligibility requires graduation from an
  accredited program.
- Pre-program applicants: the log demonstrates the depth of patient
  care experience beyond hour counts.

## Industry Relevance

Physician Assistant Programs, Teaching Hospitals, Clinics, Specialty Practices. Clinical rotations are where programs and preceptors evaluate readiness for practice, and systematic logging with gap management distinguishes strong students. A rotation log with reflections and preceptor feedback demonstrates the self-directed clinical learning employers value.
