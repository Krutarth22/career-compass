# Case-Based Therapeutics and Prescribing Decisions

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Selecting therapy from guidelines for a diagnosed condition
2. Individualizing for comorbidities, interactions, and patient factors
3. Writing a complete and safe prescription
4. Counseling the patient and planning monitoring
5. Recognizing and managing adverse effects

## What You'll Build
A therapeutics case series of twelve cases across common conditions
(hypertension, diabetes, asthma, infections, pain, depression,
anticoagulation, and others): for each, the guideline-based therapy
options, an individualized selection with reasoning about
comorbidities, interactions checked with a reference, renal or
hepatic adjustment, a complete written prescription meeting all
elements, a patient counseling script, a monitoring plan, and one
adverse effect scenario managed, reviewed by a clinician or
pharmacist.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use current guidelines and cite them; therapy changes over time.
- Interaction checking with a reputable database is required.
- A pharmacist reviewer adds significant value if available.

## Steps
1. Select twelve conditions and write realistic case vignettes with
   comorbidities and medication lists.
2. For each, identify the guideline-recommended options and their
   evidence.
3. Individualize: consider comorbidities, interactions (checked and
   documented), organ function adjustments, cost, and preferences.
4. Write the prescription with every required element and check it
   against a prescribing checklist.
5. Write the patient counseling script covering purpose, use, adverse
   effects, and when to seek help.
6. Plan monitoring: what, when, and the action thresholds.
7. Write an adverse effect scenario for each case and the management
   response.
8. Obtain review from a clinician or pharmacist, revise, and compile
   the series with a reflection on prescribing safety.

## Extension Ideas
- Add pediatric and geriatric dosing cases.
- Add a case on deprescribing in polypharmacy.
- Add controlled substance prescribing with the applicable
  regulations.
- Practice electronic prescribing in a training system.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Guideline-based therapy selection
- Individualization and interaction management
- Complete, safe prescription writing
- Patient counseling and monitoring planning

## Credential Milestones
- Physician assistant students: pharmacology and clinical therapeutics
  are assessed throughout the didactic year and constitute a major
  portion of the national certifying examination.
- Prescriptive authority after certification requires state licensure
  and, for controlled substances, federal registration and any
  state-specific requirements.
- Continuing education in pharmacology is required for certification
  maintenance in many jurisdictions.

## Industry Relevance

Primary Care Practices, Hospitals, Urgent Care, Specialty Clinics. Physician assistants prescribe across nearly all settings, and prescribing safety is a central concern of supervising physicians and regulators. A reviewed case series with complete prescriptions and monitoring plans demonstrates the judgment those settings require.
