# Reinforced Concrete Member Design and Detailing

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Designing concrete beams, slabs, columns, and footings to code
2. Detailing reinforcement for constructability and code requirements
3. Producing reinforcement drawings and bar schedules
4. Checking serviceability: deflection and cracking
5. Coordinating with the contractor's questions

## What You'll Build
A reinforced concrete design package for one bay of a small building: a
one-way slab, a beam, a column, and a spread footing designed to the
applicable code (ACI 318 or Eurocode 2), with flexure, shear,
development length, and serviceability checks, reinforcement detailing
drawings with bar schedules, a constructability review, and answers to
three realistic contractor requests for information.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use the loads and geometry from your building frame analysis where
  possible.
- Hand calculations with a spreadsheet are the primary method; software
  is for checking.
- Detailing must respect cover, spacing, bend radii, and lap lengths from
  the code.

## Steps
1. Establish the design loads and the member geometry for the bay, and
   the material strengths.
2. Design the one-way slab: thickness for deflection control, flexural
   reinforcement, and temperature steel, with checks.
3. Design the beam: flexural reinforcement at midspan and supports,
   shear stirrups with spacing, and development and anchorage.
4. Design the column for axial load and moment with an interaction
   check, tie spacing, and slenderness consideration.
5. Design the spread footing: bearing, one-way and punching shear,
   flexural reinforcement, and dowels.
6. Check serviceability: beam deflection including long-term effects
   and crack control.
7. Produce reinforcement drawings with sections, bar marks, and a bar
   bending schedule, and run a constructability review for congestion
   and placement.
8. Write and answer three contractor requests for information (a bar
   substitution, a conflict at a joint, a cover question) and compile
   the package.

## Extension Ideas
- Design a two-way flat slab with punching shear reinforcement.
- Add a shear wall design for lateral loads.
- Model the frame in software and compare moments to your design.
- Perform a strut-and-tie design for a deep beam or corbel.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Concrete member design to code
- Reinforcement detailing and bar scheduling
- Serviceability checks
- Constructability review and RFI responses

## Industry Relevance

Structural Consulting, Building Construction, Infrastructure Design. Concrete design and detailing are fundamental to structural practice in these sectors, and detailing drawings that contractors can build from are a specific skill reviewers look for. A member package with schedules and answered RFIs shows practical, not just academic, competence.
