# Construction Estimate, Schedule, and Site Plan

## Production Workflow Mirrored
1. Taking off quantities from drawings and the model
2. Building a cost estimate with unit prices, productivity, and markups
3. Developing a critical-path schedule with resources and logic
4. Planning site logistics and safety
5. Tracking progress and forecasting cost and schedule

## What You'll Build
A construction plan for the building from your document set: a
quantity takeoff, a detailed estimate with labor, material, equipment,
subcontracts, and markups, a critical-path schedule with a resource
histogram, a site logistics plan with safety considerations, and a
simulated progress update at a midpoint with earned-value forecasting.

## Student-Scope Notes
- Use published unit cost data or supplier quotes and cite them.
- Free or student scheduling software is enough; a spreadsheet CPM is
  acceptable if you show the logic.
- The progress update is simulated with plausible actuals you define.

## Steps
1. Take off quantities for the structural and site work from the
   drawings and model, organized by a standard work breakdown.
2. Build the estimate: crews, productivity rates, unit costs, material
   quantities with waste, equipment, subcontract allowances, general
   conditions, and markups.
3. Develop the schedule: activities from the breakdown, durations from
   productivity, logic ties, and the critical path.
4. Resource-load the schedule and level it to a realistic crew size,
   noting the effect on duration.
5. Draw the site logistics plan: access, laydown, crane or equipment
   placement, temporary facilities, and safety zones with a hazard
   analysis for the riskiest activity.
6. Simulate a progress update at the midpoint with actual quantities
   and costs, and compute earned value, variances, and forecasts.
7. Identify a delay and a change, and show their effect on schedule and
   cost with a recovery plan.
8. Write the construction plan document with the estimate summary,
   schedule, logistics plan, and the update analysis.

## Extension Ideas
- Add a risk register with cost and schedule contingencies.
- Perform a constructability review that feeds back to the design.
- Build a cash-flow curve and financing need.
- Compare two construction methods on cost and schedule.

## Skills Demonstrated
- Quantity takeoff and cost estimating
- Critical-path scheduling and resource leveling
- Site logistics and safety planning
- Progress measurement and forecasting

## Industry Relevance

General Contractors, Construction Management Firms, Infrastructure Owners. Civil engineers who move into construction in these sectors are hired on estimating and scheduling ability, and interviews often include a takeoff or a schedule logic exercise. A complete construction plan with a progress update demonstrates the whole skill set.
